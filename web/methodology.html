Evidence discipline

Facts come from data, not model memory

Numerical metrics come from configured data providers. Deep analysis also uses an evidence packet containing available filing metadata, news headlines, estimate revisions, earnings history and macro observations.

Missing evidence lowers confidence. Warren should not invent what a filing or article says when the underlying content was not retrieved.

The three Warren verdicts

These deliberately map to the familiar Buy / Hold / Sell mental model without assuming the user already owns the stock.

Attractive

Closest analogue: Buy

The current combination of business quality, valuation and risk/reward is sufficiently favorable to merit serious consideration.

Watch

Closest analogue: Hold / Wait

The thesis is credible, but valuation, unresolved risk, incomplete evidence or a mixed setup keeps the opportunity from being compelling today.

Avoid

Closest analogue: Sell / Do not initiate

Current business deterioration, valuation, balance-sheet weakness, structural risk or evidence quality makes the risk/reward unattractive.

Confidence is separate from the verdict. High / Medium / Low confidence describes the strength and completeness of the evidence behind Warren's current conclusion—not the probability of a positive stock return.

DCF: transparent assumptions, not a magic number

Discounted cash flow is one valuation lens. The calculation should be deterministic and its assumptions visible. Warren can explain or challenge assumptions, but the LLM does not perform free-form valuation arithmetic.

A DCF estimate is a model output, not a prediction of where the stock price will trade. Small changes in discount rate and terminal growth can materially change fair value, so Ask Warren should show sensitivity rather than false precision.

What would change Warren's view?

A useful thesis must be falsifiable. Every Deep analysis should identify specific developments that could move the verdict between Attractive, Watch and Avoid.

Examples

  • valuation entering or leaving a reasonable range;
  • sustained margin deterioration or recovery;
  • material earnings-estimate revisions;
  • liquidity, leverage or refinancing changes;
  • regulatory outcomes or moat deterioration;
  • new filings or earnings evidence that changes the thesis.

Missing evidence matters

Warren currently has important evidence limitations, including full filing text, complete licensed article content and deeper peer/industry history. These gaps should be visible through source status and confidence instead of hidden.

Methodology evolves

Scores, thresholds and valuation models should improve through evidence and evaluation—not by making outputs sound more convincing.

Material changes to weights, factor definitions, DCF assumptions, evidence rules or verdict logic should create a new methodology version so historical analyses remain interpretable.

Ask Warren is for informational and research purposes only and does not provide personalized financial advice. Current scores are heuristic and require further calibration and out-of-sample validation.
